Where it happens

Rate finds the intensity; absolute counts find the money. Bars above the company average are shown in red.

Sales Representatives lose 39.8% of their people — the role turns over its headcount roughly every two and a half years. Research & Development has the lowest rate (13.8%) yet supplies 133 of the 237 leavers, because it holds two thirds of the workforce.

The strongest driver

Overtime beats every other factor tested — including job role — on the chi-square test (87.6, p < 10⁻²⁰).

The 416 employees working overtime are 28.3% of headcount but produce 127 of the 237 leavers — 53.6% of all attrition.

2.9× the risk

A clean pay gradient: attrition falls almost nine-fold from the lowest band to the highest. Leavers earned $4,787 a month on average against $6,833 for stayers.

Risk is multiplicative, not additive

Overtime layered on any other weakness turns attrition into a coin flip.

In the worst cell — overtime workers earning under $2,500 a month — an employee is more likely to leave than to stay. The overtime + entry-level segment alone (156 people, 10.6% of headcount) produces 82 leavers: over a third of company-wide attrition.

When they leave

Ten verified insights

Every figure was computed by script and independently re-checked against the raw data before inclusion. Click any row to expand.

Overtime is the single strongest attrition driver in the dataset: 30.53% of overtime workers leave versus 10.44% of non-overtime workers (2.9x), and the 416 overtime employees (28.3% of headcount) supply 127 of 237 leavers — 53.6% of ALL attrition. Its chi-square (87.6) edges out even JobRole (86.2).

ActionMake OverTime the top-level filter on the attrition dashboard and launch an overtime-governance program (mandatory OT approval, workload rebalancing, comp-off); if the 416 OT workers left at the non-OT rate of 10.44%, expected leavers drop from 127 to ~43 — roughly 84 fewer exits, a 35% cut in total company attrition.

Risk is multiplicative, not additive: OverTime + JobLevel 1 churns at 52.56% (82 of 156) — roughly double either factor alone (JL1 26.34%, OT 30.53%) — and this 10.6%-of-headcount segment produces 34.6% of all company attrition; the closely overlapping OverTime + tenure <=2 years cell runs 50.96% (53 of 104), meaning every second overtime-loaded new joiner is lost.

ActionBuild a named 156-person watch list (OT flag AND JobLevel=1) with monthly manager review, OT caps, and accelerated level-2 promotion paths, plus a no-overtime rule for the first 12 months of employment with HR sign-off exceptions; bringing the segment to the No-OT rate of 10.44% saves ~66 of its 82 leavers, and even halving the rate saves ~41 exits per year.

The lowest income band is the concentration point and overtime turns it into near-certain churn: the <$2.5K band holds 226 employees (15.4% of 1470) yet supplies 77 of 237 leavers (32.5%) at 34.07%, and the OverTime + Income<$2.5K cell loses 48 of 70 people (68.57%) — the worst segment in the entire dataset, where an employee is more likely to leave than to stay.

ActionIntroduce a pay floor lifting the <$2.5K band into the $2.5-5K band (which runs at 16.44%) and cap or premium-pay overtime for that band, starting with the 70 sub-$2.5K overtime workers: at the 16.44% band rate their expected leavers fall from 48 to ~12 (~36 saved from adjusting only 70 salaries), and band-wide the move retains roughly 30-40 of the 77 annual leavers — likely the highest-ROI action in the analysis.

Zero equity marks nearly two-thirds of all attrition: StockOptionLevel 0 covers 631 of 1470 people (42.9%) but 154 of 237 leavers (65%), churning at 24.41% versus 9.4% at level 1 — and the marginal value is entirely in the 0-to-1 step (level 3 rebounds to 17.65%). The gap compounds with life stage: all 470 Single employees sit at level 0 and churn at 25.53%, supplying 120 of 237 leavers (50.6%), rising to 49.62% (65 of 131) when overtime is added.

ActionRoll out a broad-based level-1 equity grant (or equivalent retention bonus vesting over 2-3 years) to the 631 zero-stock employees, prioritizing the 470-person zero-stock-and-single overlap, and audit award eligibility so grants follow role and performance rather than correlating with life stage; closing even a third of the 24.41%-vs-9.4% gap saves on the order of 25-30 leavers per year.

Attrition is front-loaded to an extreme degree: first-year employees churn at 34.88% (75 of 215), so 14.6% of headcount generates 31.6% of all attrition — the steepest cliff in the tenure curve, echoed by ages 18-25 at 35.77% (44 of 123). One in three new hires never reaches year two, and recruiting and onboarding spend is written off before payback.

ActionDeploy a structured 30-60-90-day onboarding program with mandatory buddy assignment and quarterly year-one stay interviews, protected by the first-year no-overtime window; bringing the 0-1y rate down to the 2-3y band level of 18.43% saves ~35 exits per year and cuts company attrition from 16.12% to roughly 13.7%.

Three cheap frontline roles produce 60% of all leavers, and in each the leavers were the underpaid ones: Laboratory Technician (62 leavers, 23.94%, leaver median $2,603 vs stayer $3,068, 15.2% gap), Research Scientist (47 leavers, 16.1%, $2,461 vs $2,936, 16.2% gap) and Sales Representative (33 leavers, 39.76% — the worst single-role rate — $2,325 vs $2,643, 12.0% gap) together account for 142 of 237 leavers; the Sales Rep + OverTime cell loses 16 of 24 people (66.67%).

ActionCommission an external market benchmark for exactly these three roles, bring below-median incumbents to role median (targeting the 12-16% within-role gaps), and ban routine overtime for Sales Representatives; a targeted 10-15% adjustment in three roles plausibly addresses a third or more of the 142 annual leavers at the lowest salary base in the company.

The reward system provides zero retention differentiation: top performers (rating 4) leave at 16.37% (37 of 226), statistically identical to rating-3 employees at 16.08%, and salary hikes are flat across outcomes (leavers 15.1% vs stayers 15.23% mean, median 14% for both, p=0.614) — the company loses 37 documented top-rated employees a year while paying flight risks and stars the same raise.

ActionRestructure the merit cycle to a differentiated matrix (8-10% baseline, 18-20% for rating-4 employees in high-attrition roles) funded by trimming raises in near-zero-attrition top income bands, and grant level-1 stock to rating-4 staff currently at level 0 (24.41% vs 9.4%); this directly targets and could preserve 15-20 of the 37 annual top-talent losses at flat total budget.

Satisfaction metrics are threshold effects, not gradients — the signal lives entirely at the bottom score: EnvironmentSatisfaction 1 churns at 25.35% (72 of 284, contributing 30% of all attrition) while levels 2-4 cluster at 13.45-14.98%; JobInvolvement 1 shows the steepest spread at 33.73% (28 of 83) vs 9.03% at level 4; WorkLifeBalance 1 runs 31.25% (25 of 80) while the 'best' score 4 actually churns higher than 3 (17.65% vs 14.22%). Broad wellbeing programs nudging averages upward will not move attrition.

ActionReplace blanket engagement spend with targeted triage of the bottom-score populations only — manager-led root-cause interviews and 90-day fix plans for the 284 EnvSat=1 (~29 savable exits), role-redesign conversations within 30 days for the 83 JobInvolvement=1 (~12 savable), and workload correction for the 80 WLB=1 (~14 savable).

Promotion stagnation is a retention red herring while manager continuity is the real tenure-side signal: YearsSinceLastPromotion is statistically insignificant (leavers 1.95 vs stayers 2.23, p=0.199, corr -0.033) and long-stagnant veterans are the MOST loyal cohort (10y+ tenure with 7y+ no promotion: 13.79%; >7.5y promotion outliers: 12.15% — both below the 16.12% average); meanwhile leavers average 2.85 years with their current manager vs 4.37 for stayers (corr -0.156) and 2.9 vs 4.48 years in role (corr -0.161), roughly 5x stronger correlates.

ActionReallocate promotion-fairness retention budget away from long-tenured stagnant employees toward the 34.88% first-year cohort, add 'manager changes in last 24 months' as a tracked dashboard risk flag, and require HR review before reassigning employees in their first two years under a manager — same spend, redirected to where each point of improvement saves ~2 employees.

Single-KPI, rate-ranked reporting systematically misallocates leadership attention: the 16.12% headline conceals an 8.3x internal spread (68.57% in the worst cell vs 8.24% for No-OT JobLevel>=3); rate-ranking points executives at Sales (20.63%) while R&D supplies 133 of 237 leavers (56.1% of all replacement cost) at a below-average 13.84%; and of four pay fields only MonthlyIncome discriminates (corr -0.16, p=4.43e-13) — HourlyRate (p=0.791) and MonthlyRate (p=0.565) are pure noise, as is gender (p=0.291).

ActionRedesign the BI deliverable around risk tiers instead of one KPI: a tiered heat map (OT status x JobLevel x income band) benchmarked against the 8.24% No-OT senior segment, dual rate-plus-absolute-count views per department, alerting when any segment exceeds 2x the company rate, and dropping HourlyRate, MonthlyRate, and gender as retention KPIs — converting the dashboard from descriptive to operational.

The plan

Sequenced by speed to impact — governance first, then targeted compensation, then structure.

Quick wins

0-3 months
  • Impose mandatory overtime approval and caps for JobLevel 1 staff and build the named 156-person OT+JobLevel-1 watch list with monthly manager review — normalizing this 52.56% segment even halfway saves ~41 exits per year.
  • Institute a no-overtime policy for the first 12 months of employment (HR sign-off for exceptions) — bringing the OT + tenure<=2y rate of 50.96% (53 of 104) to the no-OT rate of 10.44% saves ~42 exits.
  • Ban routine overtime for Sales Representatives immediately — cutting the 66.67% OT sub-segment (16 of 24) to the role base rate of 39.76% saves ~6 exits and protects the 83-person feeder pipeline for Sales Executive.
  • Launch bottom-score triage lists for the 284 EnvSat=1, 83 JobInvolvement=1, and 80 WLB=1 employees with manager 1:1s and 90-day fix plans — worth roughly 29, 12, and 14 savable exits respectively.
  • Adopt a 'no employee at zero trainings' rule with an automated HRIS report by Q3 — moving the 54-person zero-training cohort from 27.78% to the one-training rate of 12.68% saves ~8 exits for ~54 training seats.
  • Rebuild the attrition dashboard now: OverTime as the top-level filter, dual rate-plus-count views, alerting at 2x the 16.12% company rate, and retire HourlyRate (p=0.791), MonthlyRate (p=0.565), and gender (p=0.291) as retention KPIs.

Medium term

3-9 months
  • Introduce a pay floor lifting the 226-person <$2.5K band into the $2.5-5K band (16.44% attrition), prioritizing the 70 OT+<2.5K employees — their 48 expected leavers fall to ~12 (~36 saved), with 30-40 of the band's 77 annual leavers retainable overall.
  • Commission an external market benchmark and within-role compression fixes for Laboratory Technician, Research Scientist, and Sales Representative (leaver pay gaps 15.2%, 16.2%, 12.0%) — plausibly addressing a third or more of their 142 combined annual leavers.
  • Extend a level-1 equity grant or 2-3-year vesting retention bonus to the 631 zero-stock employees, prioritizing the 470 zero-stock singles at 25.53% — closing a third of the 24.41%-vs-9.4% gap saves ~25-30 exits per year.
  • Restructure the merit cycle into a differentiated matrix (8-10% baseline, 18-20% for rating-4 employees in high-attrition roles), funded by trimming top-band raises — directly targeting the 37 annual rating-4 losses that flat 14% median hikes currently fail to prevent.
  • Deploy the structured 30-60-90-day onboarding program with buddy assignment and quarterly year-one stay interviews — cutting first-year attrition from 34.88% to the 2-3y band's 18.43% saves ~35 exits and lowers company attrition to ~13.7%.
  • Cap consecutive travel assignments, rotate frequent-travel duty across the 1,043-person rare-travel pool, and go virtual-first for the 96 single frequent travelers — moving the 277 frequent travelers from 24.91% to the rare-travel rate of 14.96% saves ~28 exits.
  • Offer hybrid days or a transport allowance specifically to the 444 employees commuting 11+ km (blended ~21% rate, 93 leavers) — reaching the 6-10 km rate of 14.47% saves up to ~29 exits, ~10 even at one-third effect.

Strategic

9+ months
  • Publish a defined 18-24-month JobLevel 1-to-2 progression framework with skill milestones and mid-cycle promotion windows — closing a quarter of the 26.34%-vs-9.74% level gap retains ~22 additional employees per year.
  • Institutionalize manager continuity: track 'manager changes in last 24 months' as a standing risk flag and require HR review before reassigning employees in their first two years under a manager (leavers average 2.85 vs 4.37 years with current manager, p=1.19e-10).
  • Permanently reallocate retention spend away from proven-safe populations — the 114 earners above the $16,581 fence (4.39% attrition) and long-stagnant veterans (13.79%, promotion-lag p=0.199) — into the early-tenure and sub-$2.5K segments where rates run 34-69%.
  • Build an early-career retention track (12/24-month career-path conversations, mentor pairing, accelerated first-promotion review) for the 729 employees aged 35 and under who supply 67.5% of all attrition — a 3-point reduction across the group saves ~22 exits per year.
  • Launch a succession and knowledge-transfer program triggered at 20 years of tenure or age 55 — full coverage of the 66 employees at 21+ years (12.12% attrition) and 47 aged 56-60 (17.02%) is low-cost and converts unplanned senior exits into planned transitions.
  • Make tiered risk-segment reporting (OT status x JobLevel x income band, benchmarked to the 8.24% No-OT senior rate) the permanent BI standard so the 8.3x internal risk spread stays visible to leadership and resource allocation follows the fire, not the average.
